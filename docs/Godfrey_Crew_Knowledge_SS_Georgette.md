# SS Georgette — Crew and Passengers of the Final Voyage, for Captain Godfrey (MetaHuman Knowledge Base)

What Captain John Godfrey would have known about the people aboard on the voyage of 1 December 1876 — officers, crew, and passengers — who they were, and what happened during the wreck itself, which he witnessed and directed first-hand. This document stops at the immediate aftermath of the wreck; it does not include the Marine Court of Inquiry's findings, certificates suspended or cancelled, arrests, awards, or anything about what became of these people afterward — none of that is something Godfrey, frozen at the moment of the wreck, would know.

Open items are flagged **[UNRESOLVED]** where the record is genuinely ambiguous or conflicting.

**Primary source for this document:** *The Herald* (Fremantle), 9 December 1876, "Loss of the 'Georgette,'" which reproduces a full published list of passengers and crew — Trove article ID 106307431 (https://trove.nla.gov.au/newspaper/article/106307431). This resolved several names and roles that earlier drafts of this document had flagged as unresolved, and is treated below as the most authoritative single source found to date.

---

## Total Complement

72 souls aboard on the final voyage: **22 crew and 50 passengers** (of the passengers, per Sub-Inspector Morby's sworn inquiry testimony: 27 men, 13 women, 10 children). The Herald's 9 December list below accounts for 21 crew by name (plus Godfrey himself as master, making 22) and around 43–45 named passengers — close to the full 50, with the remainder likely unlisted infants ("infant children, number unknown," per the same report).

---

## Ship's Company (Crew) — Complete List

**Master — John Godfrey.**

**Mate (1st Mate) — William Dundee.** Godfrey's second-in-command. During the capsize of the lifeboat, Godfrey ordered the boat slacked away astern; this wasn't done quickly enough, and the boat went in under the ship's counter and was stove in. Dundee survived and went on in the gig.

**2nd Mate — John Dewar.** When the lifeboat capsized, Godfrey ordered Dewar overboard to swim to those in the water; he went immediately, and with the Dempster brothers saved four people struggling in the water. Survived, went on in the gig, which came ashore at **Injidup** — not Calgardup Bay.

**Engineer — William Sinclair.** In the hours before the grounding, Sinclair noticed the bilge water rising faster than ordinary leakage should account for, with the pumps labouring harder than expected, and went up on deck to report it to Godfrey directly.

**2nd Engineer — J. Homeigan** (also recorded Haurigan/Houragan/Horrigan across different sources — treat "Homeigan," from the Herald's own printed list, as the best-attested spelling).

**Steward — W. Horton.**

**2nd Steward — Richard Crince.**

**Stewardess — A. Brennan.** (Earlier drafts of this document had Brennan as a male steward; the Herald's printed list is explicit — stewardess, not steward.)

**Cook — Jno. Brown.**

**A.B. Seamen (eight):** A. McLeod, Jno. Schroeder, Robt. Munnice, Thos. McGovern, Jas. Hearns, W. Cameron, A. Brand, V. Impier.

- **Archibald McLeod** — an experienced hand, by one account already on his third shipwreck. Listed plainly as an able seaman on the Herald's roster, which settles an earlier discrepancy: Godfrey's own report had separately described an "A. McLeod" in the lifeboat as "Chief Officer," but every other source, including this one, has him as an ordinary A.B. — treat "A.B. Seaman" as correct.

**Firemen (three):** Jno. Wilson, Emanuel Johames, Thos. Nunan.

**Boys (two):** Jas. Noonan, Jas. Place.

- **James Noonan** — around seventeen. When the lifeboat capsized, followed the Dempster brothers overboard and helped pull people from the water. **[UNRESOLVED]** A separate contemporary passenger list books a "Noonan, Jas." as travelling on to South Australia rather than as crew — he may have worked his passage informally. Also worth noting: this crew list includes a *separate* "Thos. Nunan, fireman" — a different person from Noonan the boy, despite the near-identical surname; don't conflate them.
- **James Place** — the boy's first name is given as "Jas." (James) on this 1876 list. **[UNRESOLVED]** A 1936 obituary elsewhere in the project archive calls him "Thomas Place" — worth checking whether that's a mistake made sixty years later, a middle name, or a different person entirely.

**[UNRESOLVED] "Scotty"** — named in Annie Simpson's 1919 letter as an able seaman distinct from 2nd Mate Dewar (both named in the same sentence, ruling out "Scotty" as a nickname for Dewar). No "Scotty" appears on the Herald's full crew roster above — he may correspond to one of the eight named A.B.s under a nickname, most plausibly one of the Scottish-sounding names (Schroeder, McGovern, Cameron, Brand), but there's no way to confirm which.

**[UNRESOLVED] "J. Lemon," stoker** — appears in a separate inquiry-witness list held elsewhere in the project archive, but does not appear anywhere on the Herald's 9 December roster of 21 crew. Worth checking whether "Lemon" is a mishearing/misprint of one of the three firemen actually listed (Wilson, Johames, Nunan), or a genuinely separate person who joined later or was omitted from this list in error.

**Resolved — "Hobbes":** earlier drafts of this document flagged an unidentified "Hobbes" appearing once in Godfrey's own report with no role given. The Herald's full list clears this up: **H. Hobbs** was a steerage **passenger**, not crew — see below.

### Children

## Naming Resolutions and Remaining Discrepancies

This 9 December list, being the fullest primary source found, resolves several things earlier drafts of this document had flagged:

- **"Hall"** is confirmed as **Miss C. Hall** — very likely the "Carrie Hall" of Annie Simpson's much later 1919 letter.
- **H. Y. Le Mesurier** is confirmed as a real steerage passenger, not just a partially-legible name.
- **"Haxwell"** is the spelling used in the Herald's own detailed list (both for the mother and the two named children), which favours van Zeller's "Hauxwell" over the "Haynes" spelling used in the project's earlier manifest-reconciliation document — though note van Zeller's thesis also names a third child, John Hauxwell (age 2), not listed by name in this Herald report (he may be among the "infant children, number unknown").

Genuine open questions this list creates or leaves standing:

- **Mrs Davis vs Mrs Davies.** The preliminary telegrams (2–5 December) and the death toll consistently name "Mrs Davis and child" among the drowned. This 9 December steerage list separately includes a "Mrs Davies" as a passenger, and a "Alexander Davis, 9 yrs" under Children — spelled without the "e." It's unclear whether Davis and Davies are the same family (a spelling slip within one article) or two different families who happened to share a very similar name.
- **Herbert Osborne is entirely absent from this list.** He does not appear among crew, saloon passengers, steerage passengers, or named children — despite being named in the preliminary 2–3 December telegrams ("Mrs Osborne, Mrs Summers and five children... Mr H. Osborne's child") and despite van Zeller's thesis placing him among the dead with a named body-recovery scene. His total absence from what otherwise looks like a comprehensive manifest is itself notable, and doesn't resolve the earlier seven-vs-eight death toll question — if anything it adds a third possibility: that "Osborne" was a name that appeared in early, confused telegraphic reporting and was quietly dropped once fuller information came in, rather than a passenger who definitely survived or definitely died.
- **Mrs Jane Weeks and infant Fredy** likely correspond to the "Frederick Augustus Weekes, age 3" mentioned elsewhere in the project archive as a survivor — but the spelling ("Weeks" vs "Weekes") and the age ("infant" vs "age 3") don't quite match; worth reconciling before treating them as confirmed the same family.

## What Happened That Night (as Godfrey experienced it)

- Early Friday morning, the Georgette sprang a leak; by about 7am there were eight feet of water in the engine room. Pumps were overwhelmed; passengers and crew bailed with buckets while she headed for shore, then about twenty miles off.
- Sinclair had already reported the rising bilge water to Godfrey; the pumps could not keep pace.
- The lifeboat was lowered, filled with passengers, but fouled against the vessel and was swamped/stove in.
- Dewar and the Dempster brothers went into the water and saved four of those struggling there; the gig was then got ready and, that night, landed survivors at **Injidup**. John Dewar was in that gig. **He did not come ashore at Calgardup Bay** (the wreck / pinnace landing). An older wording “Quinninup, about three miles from the wreck” is superseded.
- The Georgette herself beached about eight miles south of Wall Cliff, at a place called Kaldagup, around 3pm that Friday, and those still aboard were safely landed there.
- Deaths: the Court of Inquiry and Leake's letter give a final figure of seven — two women, five children. Early reports on the day named Ada Dixon, Mrs Davis, and five or six children as feared lost; van Zeller's thesis gives eight, including Herbert Osborne, who is absent from the Herald's detailed passenger list (see above) — unresolved.
- The schooner *Ione* made two attempts to reach the wreck from Bunbury and the Vasse; she couldn't get in due to heavy seas, but on her return brought in several survivors who had made it ashore from the gig at **Injidup**, including James Johns, W. Trainer, Patrick Coulan, Thomas Weston, W. Gill, John Dewar, A. McLeod, and James Noonan.
