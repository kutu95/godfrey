# SS Georgette — Busselton inquiry, for live use

Distilled from the typed transcript of the 1876–77 Formal Inquiry at Busselton Courthouse (`docs/georgette inquiry transcript (2).pdf`). That PDF is archive. This file is what the live model may retrieve.

`verified-facts.md` still outranks this file. Where they differ, follow verified-facts.

Godfrey speaks in early 1877, shortly after the board. He does not know later medals, books, or anyone's life after that year.

**Transcript caveat.** The source is a modern typing of handwriting. It has `xxx` gaps, duplicated passages, and one sitting's findings copied again in a garbled form that **reverses** Charge 3. Use the first sitting as written on 26 December (he **was** justified in putting people in the lifeboat). Do not use the later duplicate that says he was not.

Do not confuse this board with the **1873** Murray Reef inquiry (Captain Wilson).

## The court

Formal inquiry under **28 Vic No. 2**. Sittings at **Busselton Courthouse**. Clifton's office is Acting Superintendent of Customs **at the Vasse**. Both names are period-correct.

- **W. Pearce Clifton** — presiding
- **John Brockman JP**
- **G. A. Forsyth** — Harbour Master
- **William Eldridge** — Marine Engineer and Nautical Assessor

**There was no counsel.** Each man cross-examined in person. Godfrey declined to call witnesses.

---

## Two sittings (do not collapse them)

**26 December 1876 — stranding, and five charges against Godfrey.**  
The board found that she began leaking on the afternoon of 30 November rounding Cape Naturaliste; there was **insufficient evidence** of where the leak began or what caused it. After midnight the leak increased rapidly. By **4 a.m. on 1 December** there was no alternative but to run for the shore; **stranding was necessary** to save those still aboard.

On the five charges:

1. **Hasty, violent loading at Bunbury** — no evidence; no injury to the ship proved thereby.
2. **Boats insufficient / unseaworthy** — boats were **seaworthy** when she left Fremantle.
3. **Placing passengers in a leaky lifeboat** — although the boat was leaky, he was **justified** in putting people in as a precaution. The capsize and the deaths were **accidental**, and **not caused by the leaking of the boat**.
4. **Chief officer without a certificate** — Dundee **did** hold a **local certificate of competency** (lost with the ship).
5. **Pumps damaged / not in working order** — the typed finding is **damaged**. Do not quote Charge 5 as settled wording.

The same sitting also recorded opinions later tried as certificate cases: Godfrey should have done more at **8 p.m.** with a view to **putting back**; Sinclair should have reported the water more plainly; Horrigan should have found the cause of the increase; Dundee disobeyed in not keeping the lifeboat astern. Certificates of Godfrey, Dundee, Sinclair and Horrigan were **retained pending the Governor**, then the second inquiry sat.

**30 December 1876 – 2 January 1877 — certificates.**  
Charges against Godfrey, Sinclair, Horrigan and Dundee. Findings below. **17 January 1877:** Caroline Hale deposed.

Do not say the inquiry cleared him. Five of the first sitting's charges went his way. The second sitting still took his ticket.

## Findings (use this wording)

**Godfrey** — "we find Captain Godfrey guilty of neglect of duty in not ascertaining the condition of the ship and of a grave error of judgement in not putting back when it was practicable. And therefore the judgement of the court is that the certificate of Captain Godfrey be suspended for 18 months."

The board's reasons: about **8 p.m.** Sinclair reported more water than usual, the bilge pump troublesome, and that the **deck pumps** should be got ready — an **unusual** report. Godfrey had the poop pump tried and ordered it tried again at **10** and **11.30**. He did **not** go below himself to learn the depth, the rate of increase, or whether the bilge pump was doing its work. Had he known the true state between **8 and 10 p.m.**, his right course would have been to **put back** — Cape Naturaliste then under the lee, **15 to 25 miles**.

The second guilty finding is **"not putting back when it was practicable"**. It is **not** "placing passengers in the lifeboat". Charge 3 of the first sitting had already gone his way on that.

**Sinclair** — "whereupon we acquit William Sinclair of the charge of neglect of duty and return to him his certificate."  
The board found that he **did** report more water than usual, arising from the bilge pump working badly.

**Horrigan** — "we find the Second Engineer Joseph Horrigan guilty of neglect of duty in not having taken any measures to ascertain the cause of the increase of water in the ship during the first watch of November 30… the certificate of the Second Engineer Joseph Horrigan be suspended for 12 months."  
He knew the water was up when he took over at 8 p.m. and that it kept rising. He did not report the increase to the officer of the watch. The engine was not stopped. He did not test which way water was flowing through the **cock in the bulkhead** between engine-room and forehold.

**Dundee** — "Findings – guilty of disobedience but not willful, rather incompetence."  
The charge as tried: on the morning of 1 December he disobeyed in **not veering / slacking the lifeboat astern** when ordered, loss of life resulting. A garbled charge heading says "not entering the lifeboat"; **he did get in**. Use the veering finding.

## What the board heard of the night (speak this; lock counts elsewhere)

Sequence and names of the dead stay in verified-facts (T-101–T-110, F-113–F-116). In short, as sworn:

Sailed Fremantle **29 November** for Adelaide; Bunbury and the Vasse on the **30th**; left the Vasse **9.30 a.m.** Complement **72** (Wesley's certified manifest: ship's company 22; passengers 27 men, 13 women, 10 children). Missing and supposed drowned, eight names on that paper — use verified-facts spellings (**Hauxwell**, not Huxtable/Hayworth; Ada Dixon, not her mother). Wesley's paper is the wreck complement, not who joined at Bunbury. Do not invent a Bunbury boarding list (verified-facts F-109a).

About **8 p.m. 30 November** Sinclair told Godfrey the **bilge pump** was troublesome and she was making a little more water than usual because she was deeper laden; he suggested getting the **deck pumps** ready. He did not think there was cause for alarm. Godfrey asked if there was. He had the poop pump tried; it brought **no water**. He did not go below himself that evening.

Water gained in the **engine-room**. Fires out toward dawn. Head for the land. **Still miles offshore** (Godfrey: about **20 miles**, Leeuwin sand patch; Connor: about 25, sandpatch / Leeuwin in sight) the **lifeboat** was lowered, filled mainly with women and children, slacked astern, stove, and capsized. **Seven drowned in open water.** The **gig** picked Dewar and the cabin boy off the upturned boat and could not regain the steamer. The **pinnace** could not have been lowered in time to take people from the water.

**Later that day** she was run ashore at **Calgardup**. Geddes: grounded about **3.10 p.m.**; landing by **pinnace** "with difficulty, but **without loss of life**." Baling went on until she struck. Grace Bussell and Sam Isaacs are barely in this transcript; do not invent inquiry praise of the riders.

**Dundee swore** the gig came in "about a mile south of Cape Naturaliste about nine o'clock" with **14 souls**. That landing is **Injidup**, about **ten at night**, about **fifteen miles south of Naturaliste** (verified-facts F-106a, F-118). Do not adopt Dundee's mile and nine o'clock.

## Godfrey's own account (as he swore)

He had been master about **a month**, previously about **five months** chief mate. At Sinclair's 8 p.m. report he saw **no cause for alarm**. He had the deck pump rigged. He asked if he should put her under sail for a couple of hours; Sinclair said it was not necessary.

When the leak was known he thought the ship was going down. He ordered the lifeboat, put women and children in, intending to **tow her until the ship sank**. If he had done wrong he asked the court to treat it as **error of judgement**, not negligence.

Of the capsize: he made the lowering side the lee as well as he could. McLeod and another were in her first; she towed astern; she was hauled to the port side; more women put in; he ordered **Dundee** in, told him the ship was going fast, and to keep clear when she went down. He shouted **slack the boat astern** more than once or the people would drown. Dundee, in the bow with the painter, did not slack to Godfrey's knowledge. She sheared in, cracked alongside; people scrambled; she capsized. If the order had been obeyed at once he believed she would have dropped clear. Dundee had been **two days** in the ship. After the accident Dewar asked to take the boat; Godfrey suggested he jump; Dewar and **Noonan** went without orders.

Cross-examined by Dundee, Godfrey said Dundee did not go into the boat at once — he went for tobacco. He would not swear the boat was improperly steered. He believed the capsize after the strike was people getting to one side; they seemed frightened. Dundee was **not**, to his knowledge, in liquor. Dewar swore Godfrey took **no liquor at all** that night.

## What others swore (do not treat conflicts as settled)

**Sinclair.** Compound engines, surface condensing; he recalled **55** NHP — use **48** from Lloyd's if asked her power (verified-facts F-104). Bilge pump off the main engine; choked on ash, straw and rubbish; valves not tight; new ones intended at Adelaide. Sea cocks, he said, could not have flooded the bilge unless a pipe was fractured. He thought for a time the leak was **forward**, left the **fore bulkhead cock open**, and opened an **after sluice** so the deck pump might draw. He could not find a leak in the engine-room. The board still found **insufficient evidence** of where she leaked. Godfrey may say the board heard of rusted or unworkable isolation; he must not lecture on valves unless asked.

**Dundee.** Joined Fremantle **28 November**; never before chief officer. Cargo already in; timber to within inches of the beams. First knew of water about **3 a.m.** when Horrigan asked him to clear a hole in the bulkhead to the lazarette; **18 inches to 2 feet** there. Called the captain at **2.20** (ordered for 2). Three deck pumps besides the engine-room pump, he said (Sinclair named **two** — disputed, D-102). About **2 feet** of water in the lifeboat when he got in (Connor swore there was **none** when she was first dropped — disputed, D-103). He held the painter; he said he **did** slack when ordered. Fourteen in the gig.

**Dewar.** No certificate. No sounding pipe in her. Lifeboat began to leak as she touched the water. Heard "slack away"; it was **not immediately obeyed**. If it had been, she would have dropped clear. Offered to go in because the mate was slow coming.

**McLeod.** Stern oar. Boat thumped the side lowering and leaked. Gunwale got under the moulding and she filled; then under the counter and capsized. He did not see Dundee in her. He thought she would have reached the land but for that. Rose of the lazarette pump choked with mud.

**Horrigan.** Denied that any passenger remarked extra water to him after 8 p.m. (Geddes said he had). Denied hammering in the engine-room. No sudden stoppage of the screw. In defence: not the slightest cause for alarm that watch; he attended the pump as faithfully as any man could.

**Geddes.** More water in the bilge and in the **shaft tunnel** at 8.30 p.m.; told Horrigan; Horrigan said it always made that noise when she rolled. About **six feet** in the engine-room by morning. Lifeboat about **9 o'clock**, about **20 miles** off. After the capsize, every exertion to pick people up; a lookout aloft for the gig. Rumours of unfair play among the officers afterwards; he **attached no importance**. That is the inquiry's scuttling talk: looked into, **no finding of scuttling**.

**Connor.** Part-owner, senior partner Connor & McKay; management in the hands of the Fremantle agent; insured at Melbourne for **£1,000**; owner of the timber. Iron steamer, Glasgow 1872, **211 tons** (net). Consulted about getting the boats out. Helped drop the lifeboat; swore **no water in her** at that moment. Heard Godfrey shout to slack or they would drown everyone.

**Lambe.** Assisted women into the boat; they were baling; water up toward the seats. Would have preferred to stay with the ship. Heard hammering from the engine-room in the night; Horrigan denied any. Poole later spoke to him about that evidence.

**Onslow** (Lloyd's surveyor) and **Storey** (master shipwright). Surveyed her the day she sailed. Lifeboat in very good condition (not put in the water). Pinnace caulked along the keel before he passed her. Gig leaking near the stern, repaired. He believed she had **sufficient boats as required by law**. Dundee swore she had **not** enough boat accommodation. Unresolved (D-104).

**Wesley.** Certified manifest (72 / eight missing). **Wilson** (fireman, mark). **McGovern** (shipped AB, served cook). **Brennan** (stewardess). **Poole**. **Hearn** named in the bow of the lifeboat. **Hale** (17 Jan): in the boat on the second haul-up with Mrs Welch and Mrs Simpson; water to her knees; heard "let go the painter"; climbed back to the ship; boat capsized away from the ship. **Maloney**: at the after deck pump; did not know what a painter was.
