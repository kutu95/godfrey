Version: v1.10
Date: 2026-08-29
Change summary: **Embarkation lock.** Do not invent who joined at Bunbury. Dempsters left Fremantle; Little and Owston landed at Bunbury; “of Bunbury” is hometown.

Source precedence: where Lloyd's Register specifications conflict with sworn inquiry testimony, **Lloyd's wins on the ship's measurements and machinery; sworn testimony wins on events, sequence and conduct.**

Purpose: canonical factual reference. This document outranks every other source for hard facts. Where it is silent, say so rather than inferring.

Status legend:
- `verified`: direct primary-source quotation, or strong multi-source agreement
- `probable`: likely true but resting on a single secondary source
- `disputed`: sources conflict; do not answer as settled fact
- `exclude`: known incorrect claim; never assert

## 2) Vessel Facts

**ID: F-101 — Propulsion**
Claim: The Georgette was a **screw steamer**, driven by a single propeller on a shaft running aft through a shaft tunnel. She was **not** a paddle steamer and had no paddle wheels.
Status: verified | Confidence: high
Evidence:
- SRC-T1, William Dundee (Chief Officer): "The screw shaft was not covered."
- SRC-T1, Joseph Horrigan (2nd Engineer): "There was no sudden stoppage of the screw."
- SRC-T1, Walter Geddes (passenger): "I saw there was a good deal of water in the bilge – and also in the tunnel in which the screw shaft runs"
- SRC-T1, Thomas Connor (part-owner): "the screw was scarcely working."
- SRC-TH1: "Sails set, her propeller dragging, the vessel was kept afloat by a bucket gang"
Notes: A shaft tunnel is structurally exclusive to screw propulsion. The word "paddle" occurs **zero times** in all 57 pages of `SRC-T1`. See M-101 for the contamination source.

**ID: F-102 — Rig and sail**
Claim: She was a combined steam-and-sail vessel (auxiliary steamer), carrying square canvas forward and fore-and-aft sail aft. Named sails in evidence: foresail, topsail(s), main trysail, staysail. Yards were carried forward.
Status: verified | Confidence: high
Evidence:
- SRC-T1, Dundee: "She was going about five knots under steam and sail."
- SRC-T1, Dundee: "She was under Canvas heading towards the Land under foresail, topsail, main trysail"
- SRC-T1, John Dewar: "I don't think that if the Foresail had been braced xx well aback and the main topsail braced up and the staysail sheet hauled to windward…"
- SRC-T1, Horrigan: "I lent you a hand to haul the yards around"
Rig class, per SRC-LR1 (Lloyd's survey): **two-masted topsail schooner**, masts of pitch pine, carrying foresail, fore topsail, fore topmast staysail, mainsail and main topsail. **No bowsprit** (struck through on the Lloyd's survey form).
Notes: The v1.0 instruction not to assert a rig class is **superseded** — the class is now sourced. The transcript strings "fore raft sails" / "five raft sails" / "the four aft sails" are transcription corruptions of "fore and aft sails". Dundee's "main trysail" is his own wording for her after canvas.

**ID: F-103 — Build, builder and tonnage**
Claim: Built by **McKellar, McMillan & Co** at the Lower Woodyard, **Dumbarton** on the Clyde, as that firm's **Yard No. 1** — their first vessel. **Launched 3 October 1872**, named by Miss M'Kellar of Renton. **Port of registry: Glasgow.** Classed **90 A1** at Lloyd's. **336.72 tons gross, 211.64 tons net.** Cargo capacity about 400 tons.
Status: verified | Confidence: high
Evidence:
- SRC-LR1, from Lloyd's Register survey records.
- SRC-T1, Thomas Connor: "She was an iron steamer built in Glasgow 1872 – 211 tons – xxx 6800 xxxs"
Notes: Connor's "built in Glasgow" is the **port of registry**, not the yard. Say built at Dumbarton on the Clyde, registered at Glasgow. His "211 tons" is the **net** register tonnage — the commercial and regulatory figure, and the right plain answer to "how big was she". The press figure of 213 (`SRC-NV1`) and the 210–212 range are roundings of net tonnage. Gross is 336.

**ID: F-103a — Principal dimensions**
Claim: Registered length 151 ft 5 in; registered breadth 22 ft 9 in (moulded 22 ft 4 in); registered depth 11 ft 6 in (bilge to main deck beams 11 ft 7 in). Mean laden draught about 11½ ft. Screw submerged 8 ft.
Status: verified | Confidence: high
Evidence: SRC-LR1, Lloyd's survey.
Notes: Supersedes D-105. Dundee's "about 10 feet aft" and Dewar's "about 12 foot aft" are recollections of the final passage, not registered figures.

**ID: F-103b — Hull and fittings**
Claim: Iron framed with lap-riveted (clinker) iron plating; not copper-sheathed, so requiring regular cleaning and anti-fouling. Black hull above the waterline, white topsides, black funnel, red-brown composition below, name in gilt on the bow. Two bower anchors (approx. 2,096 lb and 297 lb), one stream anchor, one kedge; 90 fathoms of 1 in chain cable and 90 fathoms of 7½ in hempen stream cable.
Status: verified | Confidence: high
Evidence: SRC-LR1, Lloyd's survey.
Notes: `SRC-LR1` calls this "composite construction". **Do not have Godfrey use the word "composite"** — conventionally it means iron frames with wooden planking, and a knowledgeable visitor will hear a wooden hull. Say iron built, iron framed, iron plated.

**ID: F-104 — Machinery**
Claim: Two-cylinder compound, inverted direct-acting engine by **Smith Brothers & Co, Kingston Engine Works, Glasgow**. High-pressure cylinder 19 in bore, low-pressure 33 in bore, 24 in stroke. **48 nominal horsepower.** One **Scotch boiler** amidships, furnace facing forward, working pressure **65 psi**, also by Smith Brothers. Surface condenser. About 96 rpm. A donkey engine and boiler in the engine room; a steam winch for hoisting timber. Bilge and feed pumps, one of each, 3¼ in bore.
Status: verified | Confidence: high
Evidence:
- SRC-LR1, Lloyd's Register engine survey — 48 NHP, cylinder bores, boiler and builder.
- SRC-T1, William Sinclair (Chief Engineer): "The engines were compound, surface condensing, 55 nominal horse power. One between main boiler in the middle of the ship – furnace facing forward."
- SRC-T1, Sinclair: "The boiler and machinery were in average condition and worked with 40 to 53 steam"
Notes on the horsepower conflict — **three figures exist**: 48 NHP (Lloyd's survey), 55 NHP (Sinclair, sworn), and 60 NHP "capable of being worked up to 260" (*Herald*, Fremantle, 13 Sep 1873). Under the precedence rule, **use 48**; Sinclair was recalling a figure in the witness box. Godfrey may cite "up to 260 at full power" as the popularly reported figure. On the boiler, 65 psi is the design working pressure and Sinclair's "40 to 53" is what she was actually carrying on the final passage — both true, not a conflict.

**ID: F-104a — Propeller, coal and speed**
Claim: Single screw, 4 solid blades, 8 ft 6 in diameter, 11 ft 6 in pitch, on a shaft 36 ft long. A **spare propeller stowed on deck**, uncommon in a vessel her size. Bunkers 60 tons for coastal service; burns about 4 tons per day. Ordinary speed about 7 knots.
Status: verified | Confidence: high
Evidence: SRC-LR1 (Lloyd's survey; 7 knots from the 1873 Court of Inquiry; 4 tons/day from Captain Macfarlane's delivery-voyage report).
Notes: `SRC-LR1` gives the shaft as "approx. 3½ in diameter", the same bore it gives for the bilge pump two lines earlier. **This looks like a transcription slip and is flagged for the author** — do not have Godfrey quote a shaft diameter. On the final passage she made about 5 knots from Bunbury until about 8 p.m., then 7 knots by patent log (SRC-LR1), consistent with Dundee's sworn "about five knots under steam and sail".

**ID: F-105 — Pumps**
Claim: The bilge pump was worked off the main engine and choked repeatedly on coal ash, straw and rubbish; its valves were not tight and replacements were to be fitted at Adelaide. There were deck pumps in addition, one on the poop and one on the main deck alleyway. There was **no sounding pipe** aboard for measuring water in the hold, and **no carpenter** in the ship's company.
Status: verified | Confidence: high
Evidence:
- SRC-T1, Sinclair: "the Georgette had a bilge pump worked by and connected with main engine"
- SRC-T1, Sinclair: "It arose from the ashes, straw and rubbish getting up thro the pipe into the valves… The valves were not air tight we intended to get new ones at Adelaide."
- SRC-T1, Dewar: "There was in Georgette no sounding pipe for finding depth of water in the hold."
- SRC-T1, Dundee: "there was no carpenter on board Georgette."
- SRC-GL1: "besides the pumps were choked and useless."
Notes: The **number of deck pumps is disputed** — Dundee swears three, Sinclair two. See D-102.

**ID: F-106 — Boats**
Claim: Three boats: lifeboat, pinnace and gig. The pinnace was about the size of the lifeboat and would carry about twenty; it made the repeated crossings that landed most passengers **at Calgardup Bay**, where the ship was run ashore. The gig was 25–27 feet, old, and patched on the bilge with lead or tin. **The gig did not land at Calgardup.** After the lifeboat capsize it could not return to the steamer and came ashore at **Injidup**.
Status: verified | Confidence: high
Evidence:
- SRC-T1, Dundee: "She had a lifeboat, gig and pinnace."
- SRC-T1, Dundee: "The Pinnace was about the same size as the Life Boat but would have carried about 20 people."
- SRC-T1, Dundee: "The patch on the Gig did not render her unfit to carry passengers – the patches were lead or tin – on the Bilge"
- SRC-GL1: "the other boat which unable to return to the steamer shaped its course for land, the steamer doing the same thing."
Notes: `SRC-LR1` calls the pinnace "a smaller pinnace" and records only **two** boats on an early Lloyd's entry, a third evidently added at a later refit. On boat size the sworn evidence stands. **Do not place the gig, or anyone known to have been in her, on Calgardup beach.** See F-106a.

**ID: F-106a — Who was in the gig, and where it came ashore**
Claim: **The gig came ashore at Injidup with 14 souls.** It did not land at Calgardup Bay. Dundee's police list of 3 December 1876 names them all. **Crew:** W. Dundee, 1st Mate; John Dewar, 2nd Mate; Arch. McLeod, A.B.; James Mooney, A.B. **Passengers:** J. Dempster; W. Dempster; Miss Welche; Mrs H. Dixon and 1 child; Mrs Simpson and 1 child; Mrs Stammers and 2 children. That is six men, four women, and four children. **Chief Officer William Dundee jumped into the gig after the lifeboat capsize and landed with her.** Dewar and the cabin boy were picked off the upturned lifeboat into the gig. Willie Dempster was already in her as she hung astern; James Dempster went overboard after him. **Dewar was not at Godfrey's side for the Calgardup / pinnace landing, and neither was Dundee.** Godfrey and George Leake landed later, at Calgardup, by pinnace. Do not put Dundee on that last pinnace crossing.
Status: verified | Confidence: high
Evidence:
- SRC-GM1, Dundee to Police W. Carroll, 3 Dec 1876: the fourteen names above.
- SRC-T1, Dundee: they got "four women and four children into the boat"; he "hauled up the gig and jumped into her from the stern"; "picked the two men off the lifeboat and made for the shore"; "we beached the boat and all landed in safety 14 souls in all."
- SRC-T1, Dewar: "with myself there were 14 in her"; Willie Dempster in the gig hanging astern; James Dempster, the cabin boy and Dewar jumped overboard; Dewar and the cabin boy were picked off the lifeboat by the gig.
- SRC-HR1: Dewar, McLeod and Noonan among survivors from the gig; Dundee "survived and went on in the gig."
- SRC-GL1: the other boat "unable to return to the steamer shaped its course for land."
- Author correction 2026-08-17 (installation owner): gig landing is **Injidup**, not Calgardup. SRC-HR1's "Quinninup, about three miles from the wreck site" is **superseded** for the gig's beach.
Notes: In character, say **Injidup** for the gig. Never say Redgate. Never collapse the two landings into one bay. If asked whether Dewar or Dundee landed at Calgardup, correct it: they were in the gig, at Injidup. **Mooney / Noonan:** the police paper writes the fourth crewman as James Mooney, A.B. Dewar’s cabin boy and the *Herald*’s James Noonan occupy that same seat. There is no Mooney on the *Herald* crew of 22. Do not add a fifteenth person or a twenty-third hand. Godfrey may name the boy **Noonan**. **Miss Welche** is Hale’s “Mrs Welch” and the *Herald*’s Miss M. Welsh — one passenger, not three. Do not guess that Johns, Trainer, Coulin, Weston or Gill were in this boat merely because the *Ione* later brought some survivors in.

**ID: F-106b — Annie Simpson and her infant**
Claim: **Annie Simpson, steerage, with her infant, came ashore in the gig at Injidup. She did not come ashore in the pinnace at Calgardup Bay.** She and the child both survived. She had first been put into the lifeboat; after it capsized she was among those taken into the gig. The McGregors of Quindalup, near where the gig landed, cared for her and the baby afterwards.
Status: verified | Confidence: high
Evidence:
- SRC-GM1: "Mrs Simpson and 1 child" on Dundee’s gig list.
- SRC-T1, Caroline Hale: when the lifeboat was hauled up a second time, "Mrs Welch and Mrs Simpson were" in her.
- SRC-TH1, exegesis: Annie Simpson travelled steerage; after the wreck she corresponded with the McGregors of Quindalup "near the place where the gig was landed"; they "cared for Simpson and her baby for a number of weeks."
- SRC-HR1: "Mrs O. (Annie) Simpson" on the *Herald* steerage list.
- Author correction 2026-08-20 (installation owner): she came ashore in the gig at Injidup, with her baby — not in the pinnace at Calgardup.
Notes: **Do not infer Calgardup from the fact that she survived.** Survivors of the boat went two ways: back aboard (later the pinnace), or into the gig (Injidup). Annie went with the gig. Hale climbed back to the ship and landed at Calgardup. Never say Godfrey saw Annie on Calgardup beach. In character, say **her infant** or **the Simpson child**. Later letters name the boy Harry; some later writing uses Henry. Do not have Godfrey quote those letters, Minnesota, or the *Annie Lisle*.

**ID: F-116 — How the 72 divided at the two landings**
Claim: **Ship's company 22, passengers 50, 72 souls in all.** After the lifeboat capsize, **14** went in the gig to **Injidup**. **Seven** were already lost from the boat, in open water. **About 50 remaining aboard** were landed at **Calgardup Bay** by the pinnace (Leake). The eighth death, Herbert Osborne, was aboard the ship, not in the boat; the Calgardup landing of the living is the "about 50."
Status: verified | Confidence: high
Evidence:
- SRC-T1, Wesley: 72 / 22 crew / 50 passengers (27 men, 13 women, 10 children).
- SRC-T1, Dundee and Dewar: 14 in the gig.
- SRC-GM1: the same 14 named.
- SRC-GL1: "preparations were made for landing those remaining on board about 50."
- Arithmetic: 72 − 7 lost in the boat − 14 in the gig = 51 still with the ship, including Osborne who died aboard, which leaves Leake's about 50 landed alive at Calgardup.
Notes: Give 22 crew, 50 passengers, 72 total if asked who was aboard. Give **about 50** if asked how many came ashore at Calgardup. Give **14** if asked how many were in the gig. **Do not invent a named list of the fifty at Calgardup.** Named as having landed there from the ship: Godfrey, George Leake (last pinnace crossing), Walter Geddes, Caroline Hale, Thomas Connor. Grace Bussell and Sam Isaacs were on that beach from the shore, not from the ship.

**ID: F-107 — Ownership**
Claim: Owned by the firm **Connor & McKay**. **Thomas Connor was senior partner and part-owner, not sole owner.** He travelled as a passenger on the final voyage and owned the timber cargo. Management of the vessel's affairs rested with her agent at Fremantle. Hull insured at Melbourne for £1,000; cargo and freight for about £800.
Status: verified | Confidence: high
Evidence:
- SRC-T1, Connor: "I am senior partner in the firm of Connor & McKay and part owner of the Georgette. … the management of the affairs was in the hands of her agent at Fremantle – the ship was insured in Melbourne for 1000 pounds – I was owner of the timber"
Notes: `SRC-TH1` uses the loose shorthand "the ship's owner, Thomas Connor". Do not adopt it. Name variants: `W Connor` (transcription error) and a deponent-list mislabel "Connor – crewman".

**ID: F-108 — Trade and route**

**ID: F-108a — The trade she was built for**
Claim: **Built for the French and Baltic grain trade**, and made several voyages in the French trade before her sale for service in Western Australia.
Status: verified | Confidence: high
Evidence: SRC-LR1.

**ID: F-108b — Commercial standing**
Claim: Connor & McKay hold the Western Australian government mail contract, subsidised at £4,000 a year. Purchase price £9,000, with a further £7,000–£8,000 spent on passenger accommodation (total £16,000–£17,000). Gross earnings about £1,000 a month per the 1875 prospectus. Agents: **Messrs King & Son, Fremantle**. Hull insured at £1,000 at the time of loss.
Status: verified | Confidence: medium
Evidence: SRC-LR1; SRC-T1 (Connor, on the £1,000 insurance and the Fremantle agency).
Notes: `SRC-LR1` flags a competing purchase price of £14,000 from Colonial Secretary's records via McCarthy. Use £9,000 plus refit, per the author's instruction. Connor's sworn "the management of the affairs was in the hands of her agent at Fremantle" is the same agency `SRC-LR1` names as King & Son.

**ID: F-108c — Accommodation**
Claim: Built as a cargo vessel, not for passengers. Refitted after purchase with two passenger cabins of twenty berths each and two saloons, one for ladies and one for gentlemen. Praised on her 1873 arrival for cleanliness and comfort.
Status: verified | Confidence: high
Evidence: SRC-LR1.

**ID: F-108d — The compass**
Claim: A recurring documented defect. Deviated by up to 25 degrees on some headings; first flagged at the 1873 Murray Reef inquiry and readjusted at Adelaide in August 1875. Godfrey would have known this well.
Status: verified | Confidence: high
Evidence: SRC-LR1, citing the 1873 Court of Inquiry.

**ID: F-108e — Service history before Godfrey**
Claim: Delivery voyage Glasgow to Fremantle under **Captain Macfarlane**, 12 July – 11 September 1873, 59 days non-stop without coaling or entering any port; departed with 280 tons of coal, arrived with 30; averaged about 6.4 knots and was reported as the fastest passage on record from England to Western Australia. First anchored at Sulphur Bay, Garden Island, for engine cleaning and hull scraping. **Murray Reef stranding, October 1873** — holed, rudder lost, carried clear when the engineer failed to reverse the engines and the still-turning screw with sail set took her off; steered ashore on a jury rig; **Captain Wilson** found guilty of want of due care and his certificate found to have been improperly granted. **Warnbro Sound stranding, November 1873.** Sequence of masters: Macfarlane → Wilson → **Gordon McKinnon** (popular; farewelled with 40 sovereigns and a gold chronometer, left March 1875) → **R. Shand** (died of paralysis at sea off Hamelin Bay, November 1875, near where the Georgette herself was lost a year later) → **Godfrey** (chief officer from mid-1876, master about a month before the final voyage).
Status: verified | Confidence: high
Evidence: SRC-LR1.

**ID: F-108f — The Catalpa pursuit**
Claim: In April 1876 the Georgette was sent in pursuit of the American whaleship *Catalpa* during the Fenian prisoner escape from Fremantle. **Godfrey was not aboard** — he joined her as chief officer in mid-1876, after the event.
Status: verified | Confidence: high
Evidence: SRC-LR1 (the pursuit, and Godfrey's mid-1876 joining date).
Notes: This is genuinely the Georgette's history and Godfrey should be able to speak of it as any man on the coast would — but never in the first person as a participant. **This reverses part of v1.0's M-108**, which wrongly listed the *Catalpa* among vessels never to be associated with the Georgette.

**ID: F-109 — Final voyage and cargo**
Claim: Loaded hewn and sawn jarrah at Bunbury from 17 Nov 1876 (arrived 12 Nov, delayed by weather), then steamed to Fremantle for passengers. Sailed Fremantle 29 Nov for Adelaide; called Bunbury morning of 30 Nov; left the Vasse 9.30 a.m. that day. Timber was stowed to within inches of the deck beams and passengers' luggage laid on it between the hatch combings. She was more deeply laden than witnesses had ever seen her.
Status: verified | Confidence: high
Evidence:
- SRC-T1, Dewar: "Sailed from Fremantle for Adelaide 29th November. Called in at Bunbury on the morning of the 30th… proceeded to Vasse and left Vasse for Adelaide at 9.30am."
- SRC-T1, Dundee: "She was full to within inches of the Beams. It was sawn timber"
- SRC-T1, Sinclair: "I had never before seen the Georgette loaded so deeply"
- SRC-TH1: "the inquiry transcript squarely places the loading as taking place from the 12th November, during an earlier voyage."

**ID: F-109a — Who came aboard at Bunbury**
Claim: There is **no named list** of passengers or hands who joined at Bunbury (or the Vasse) on 30 November. **Thomas Little** and **William Owston** booked only as far as Bunbury and **left her there** — they were not aboard when she was lost. **James and Willie Dempster left Fremantle with the ship**; they did not join at Bunbury. “James Lambe of Bunbury” and “Richard Flanagan of Bunbury” are **hometowns**, not embarkation. Do not name Mrs Davis, John Maloney, Thomas Lennon/Nunan, or anyone else as having come aboard at Bunbury.
Status: verified | Confidence: high
Evidence:
- SRC-HR1: Little and Owston “Booked only as far as Bunbury (disembarked before the wreck).”
- SRC-NW1 / Dempster letter as printed (*Inquirer*, 10 January 1877): “After leaving Fremantle we got on all right to here (Vass).”
- SRC-JL1: “Richard Flanagan of Bunbury, also a passenger.”
Notes: If asked who joined at Bunbury, say in character that you cannot sworn-name them. Wesley’s certified manifest is the wreck complement (F-110), not a port-by-port boarding roll.

**ID: F-112 — Date and place of loss**
Claim: Lost 1 December 1876, run ashore on the Western Australian coast at Calgardup Bay (modern Redgate Beach — never use the modern name in character). **Calgardup is the wreck and the pinnace landing. It is not where the gig came in** (Injidup — F-106a).
Status: verified | Confidence: high
Evidence: SRC-T1 depositions throughout; SRC-TH1 background section.

**ID: F-117 — Willie Dempster saved the gig**
Claim: While the lifeboat was still being filled, the **gig’s after fall or ring-bolt carried away**. She hung by the bows, filling and striking the steamer. **Willie Dempster** slid down the fall, had her lowered, and bailed her. Without that she might have been lost before she took anyone.
Status: verified | Confidence: high
Evidence:
- SRC-GL1: “in lowering this one the after fall carried away and the boat was hanging with her stern in the water and might have got away altogether had not Willie Dempster without a moment's hesitation swung himself into her”
- SRC-NW1 / Dempster letter as printed: ring-bolt broke; Willie slid down the fall and called to lower away.
Notes: Godfrey saw this from the deck. Credit Willie by name if asked how the gig was got away.

**ID: F-118 — The gig’s passage to Injidup**
Claim: After picking Dewar and Noonan off the upturned lifeboat, the gig **could not overtake the steamer**. They shaped their own course: a leaky boat, no proper sails or rudder, three oars, bailing throughout. They later set a towel, then a **blanket on an oar**. They landed about **ten at night**, about **fifteen miles south of Cape Naturaliste** (Injidup).
Status: verified | Confidence: high

## 3) The Dead

**ID: F-113 — Death toll**
Claim: **Eight lives were lost in all, women and children. Seven of them drowned in the capsize of the lifeboat. The eighth, Herbert Osborne, died aboard the ship — his body was recovered from inside her.**
Status: verified | Confidence: high
Evidence:
- SRC-T1, Charles Wesley, certified police manifest: "Of those passengers the following are missing and supposed to be drowned – Herbert Osborne / Mrs A Dixon / Mrs E Huxtable and child / Frances Hayworth / Isabella Hayworth / Mrs Davis and child / Eight souls in all"
- SRC-TH1: "resulting in eight drownings"
- SRC-GL1: "Of those in the boat when it capsized seven were drowned nearly two women and five children"
Notes: **The seven-versus-eight conflict is resolved and closed.** Leake's letter and the inquiry finding of seven count only those lost out of the lifeboat. Herbert Osborne was not in the boat: he died aboard the ship, and van Zeller records the recovery of his body from inside her, identified by the steward from the berth it had occupied and handed to a police constable named Hackett. That is why the *Herald*'s 9 Dec 1876 manifest omits him from the drowned while the police manifest includes him. Eight in all; seven from the boat.

Corroboration that the same seven people are meant by every source: the police manifest's eight is exactly the seven below **plus** Herbert Osborne, and no other name varies between the lists.

4. John Hauxwell, aged two
5. Frances Hauxwell, aged eight
6. Isabella Hauxwell, aged four

Lost aboard the ship:
8. Herbert Osborne, aged eight

Status: verified | Confidence: high
Evidence:

Georgette / Master Herbert Osborne, eight years of age / Miss Ada Dixon, eight years of age / Mrs Elizabeth Hauxwell / Master John Hauxwell, two years of age / Miss Frances Hauxwell, eight years of age / Miss Isabella Hauxwell, four years of age / Mrs. Davis and child"

- **Mrs Harriet Dixon, Ada's mother, survived** and came ashore in the gig **at Injidup** with a surviving infant (F-106a). The police manifest's "Mrs A Dixon" among the drowned is **Ada** miscopied, not her mother. This closes the v1.0 note about the Dixon adult/child question.
- **Annie Simpson and her infant** also came ashore in that gig (F-106b). Do not place them at Calgardup.
- **Ada Dixon:** death notice, *Inquirer and Commercial News*, 13 Dec 1876 — drowned 1 December from the Georgette, **fourth daughter** of Herbert and Harriet Dixon of Fremantle, aged 8; **body not recovered**. The courthouse display date “2 December” is not to be used.

- John Hauxwell is not named in the *Herald* children's list; he is likely among its "infant children, number unknown".

**ID: F-115 — Lifeboat sequence vs grounding**
Claim: **The lifeboat was lowered while the Georgette was still miles offshore — about twenty miles from land, with the Leeuwin sand patch in sight — after she had been turned for the shore but well before she was run aground.** It was filled mainly with women and children, passed astern to tow, stove under the ship's counter and capsized. **Seven drowned in that capsize, in open water.** The gig could not return to the steamer and made for land (Injidup). **Only later** was the Georgette herself run ashore at Calgardup Bay; those still aboard landed there by pinnace. The seven from the boat were already lost. They did not drown in the surf after she grounded, and the boat was not launched after she was on the beach.
Status: verified | Confidence: high
Evidence:
- SRC-GL1: "We were at this time about 20 miles from land in sight of the Leeuwin sand patch. As soon as the lifeboat was launched…" then the fill, the drop astern, the stove and capsize; "the steamer doing the same thing" (shaping course for land) after the accident.
- SRC-GL1: "Of those in the boat when it capsized seven were drowned nearly two women and five children"
- SRC-T1, Dundee and others: painter / slacking astern / stove under the counter (the capsize sequence). SRC-T1 throughout places the stranding at Calgardup as the later beaching, not the moment the boat went down.
- SRC-HR1: pumps overwhelmed while "headed for shore, then about twenty miles off"; lifeboat lowered and stove; ship herself beached later that Friday (~3 p.m. in that compilation).
Notes: **v1.3's T-106 wrongly joined "run ashore" and "lifeboat capsized" in one morning row.** That compression caused Godfrey to say the boat was lowered after she grounded. Do not restore it. Author correction 2026-08-19.

## 7) Known Misconceptions / Exclusions

**M-101 — Paddle steamer.** Never state or imply the Georgette was a paddle steamer or had paddle wheels. She was a screw steamer (F-101).
Contamination source: `SRC-NV1` p.36 describes her predecessor the *Xantho* as "that little paddle steamer" in a paragraph about the Georgette. Retrieval will surface that phrase adjacent to the Georgette. It refers to a **different vessel**. Secondary risk: the exegesis repeatedly analogises Grace Bussell to Grace Darling and the SS *Forfarshire*, a different ship again.

**M-102 — Colonial-government built.** Never state or imply the Georgette was built by the colonial government. She was built at Dumbarton on the Clyde in 1872 for private owners and came to the colony by sale (F-103).

**M-103 — WITHDRAWN in v1.1.** This entry previously excluded the Baltic grain trade claim on the grounds that no source supported it. Lloyd's Register does support it. She **was** built for the French and Baltic grain trade — see F-108a. Godfrey may state this. Do not reinstate this exclusion.

**M-103a — Glasgow as birthplace.** Do not say she was *built* at Glasgow. Glasgow is her port of registry and the home of her engine builders; the hull came off the Lower Woodyard at Dumbarton (F-103).

**M-104 — Sole ownership.** Do not call Thomas Connor the sole owner. Part-owner and senior partner of Connor & McKay (F-107).

**M-105 — Dundee as bosun.** Dundee was Chief Officer. **No bosun is named anywhere in the primary evidence**; the single "bo'sun" in the corpus is an unnamed fictional character in `SRC-NV1`.

**M-106 — Grace Bussell as sole rescuer.** Rescue involved multiple actors including Sam Isaacs and the ship's crew in the pinnace.

**M-107 — Full exoneration.** The inquiry did not clear Godfrey. Five charges acquitted, two adverse findings, certificate suspended 18 months.

**M-108 — Other vessels.** These appear in the corpus and must never be conflated with the Georgette: *Xantho*, SS *Forfarshire*, SS *Rob Roy*, barquentine *Harrison* (note: also commanded by a Captain Godfrey, also carrying Bunbury timber, also sprang a leak off Cape Naturaliste — a severe collision risk), brig *Laughing Wave*, schooner *Ione*, barque *Ashburton*, SS *Wairarapa*, *Delaware*, *Nelcebee*, *Waratah*.
The whaleship **Catalpa was removed from this list in v1.1** — the Georgette genuinely pursued her in April 1876 (F-108f). Godfrey may discuss it, but not as a participant.

**M-109 — The Catalpa in the first person.** Never let Godfrey place himself aboard for the Catalpa pursuit. He joined her afterwards (F-108f).

**M-110 — "Composite" construction.** Do not use the word "composite" of her hull, despite `SRC-LR1` using it. See F-103b.

**M-111 — Gig people at Calgardup.** Never state or imply that the gig, or anyone known to have been in her, came ashore at Calgardup Bay (including “the north end of the bay” / Black Rock). That includes **John Dewar, William Dundee, Archibald McLeod, James Noonan (police paper: Mooney), both Dempsters, Miss Welche, Harriet Dixon and her infant, Annie Simpson and her infant, and Mrs Stammers and her two children**. The gig came ashore at **Injidup**. Calgardup is the wreck and the pinnace landing (F-106, F-106a, F-106b). Do not use SRC-HR1’s Quinninup wording for the gig. Do not put Dundee on the last pinnace crossing with Godfrey and Leake.

**M-112 — Lifeboat after grounding.** Never state or imply that the lifeboat was lowered, filled, or capsized **after** the Georgette was run ashore, or that the seven drowned in the surf at Calgardup. The boat went down **miles offshore**, still well out from the land; the beaching at Calgardup was later (F-115, T-106, T-106b).

**M-113 — Dempster/Argus count errors.** Never say there were **20** in the gig, or **58** souls aboard. The Dempster letter as printed has “ten adults and ten children”; the Argus interview of the same brothers has 58 aboard. Both are wrong. Use **14** in the gig and **72** aboard (F-106a, F-110). Never say she was **scuttled** (F-121).

**M-114 — Invented embarkation.** Never assign **Fremantle / Bunbury / the Vasse** as a boarding port for a named passenger or hand unless this document states that port for that person. Do not infer boarding from hometown (“of Bunbury”). Do not say the Dempsters, Mrs Davis, John Maloney, or Thomas Lennon/Nunan joined at Bunbury (F-109a). Little and Owston **landed** at Bunbury; they did not join there.
