# SS Georgette — Ship Facts for Captain Godfrey (MetaHuman Knowledge Base)

Compiled for the Captain Godfrey AI installation. These are the facts, figures and statistics about his ship that Captain John Godfrey — who served as chief officer for roughly five months before taking command in 1876 — would have known first-hand. Sourced from the manuscript (*The Georgette: How a Shipwreck Became a Legend*, v7) and the underlying primary-source research (Lloyd's Register survey documents, the 1873 Court of Inquiry transcript, and contemporary newspaper reports via Trove).

Two figures below are flagged as **[DISCREPANCY]** where sources disagree — worth a decision on which number Godfrey should quote.

---

## Identity

- **Name:** Georgette, named by Miss M'Kellar of Renton at her launch.
- **Type:** Iron screw steamer, composite construction (iron frames, clinker/lap-riveted iron plating).
- **Rig:** Two-masted topsail schooner — foresail, fore topsail, fore topmast staysail, mainsail, main topsail. **No bowsprit** (struck through on the Lloyd's survey form).
- **Builder:** McKellar, McMillan & Co, the Lower Woodyard, Dumbarton, on the Clyde. She was **Yard No. 1** — the very first vessel the firm built.
- **Launched:** 3 October 1872.
- **Classification:** 90 A1 at Lloyd's Register.
- **Port of registry:** Glasgow.
- **Built for the French and Baltic grain trade**, and made several voyages in the French trade before her sale for service in Western Australia.
- **Current owners:** Connor & McKay, who hold the WA government mail contract.

## Principal Dimensions

- **Length (registered, waterline):** 151 ft 5 in
- **Breadth (registered):** 22 ft 9 in (moulded breadth 22 ft 4 in)
- **Depth (registered):** 11 ft 6 in (internal depth, bilge to main deck beams: 11 ft 7 in)
- **Draught, laden (mean):** 11½ ft
- **Draught at the time of the 1873 Murray Reef stranding:** 10 ft 8 in aft, 8 ft forward
- **Screw depth submerged:** 8 ft

## Tonnage

- **Gross register tonnage:** 336.72 tons (contemporary press rounded this to 336 or 337)
- **Net register tonnage:** 211.64 tons (commonly cited as 210–212 tons — this is the figure used for commercial/regulatory purposes)
- **Cargo capacity:** 400 tons of general cargo

## Engine and Machinery

- **Engine:** Two-cylinder compound, inverted direct-acting. High-pressure cylinder 19 in bore, low-pressure (condensing) cylinder 33 in bore, 24 in (2 ft) stroke.
- **Engine builder:** Smith Brothers & Co, Kingston Engine Works, Glasgow.
- **Nominal horsepower:** **[DISCREPANCY]** The Lloyd's Register engine survey (the primary technical record, and the figure used in the manuscript body) gives **48 nominal horsepower**. The *Herald* (Fremantle, 13 Sep 1873) arrival report instead states **"60 nominal horse power, but capable of being worked up to 260"** — likely a rounder, more impressive figure for a newspaper puff piece, with 260 representing peak indicated horsepower under full steam rather than nominal rating. Recommend Godfrey use 48 NHP as the technically correct figure but be able to cite "capable of working up to 260 hp at full power" as the popularly reported figure of the day.
- **Boiler:** One Scotch boiler, working pressure 65 psi, built by Smith Brothers & Co.
- **Condenser:** Surface condenser (an advance over older jet condensers — allowed feed water recycling and higher boiler pressure).
- **RPM:** 96 (engine and screw).
- **Donkey engine and boiler:** One, in the engine room, for auxiliary power (winches, pumps).
- **Bilge and feed pumps:** One of each, 3¼ in bore.

## Coal

- **Normal bunker capacity (coastal service):** 60 tons, stowed in bunkers with lids flush with the quarter deck.
- **Average consumption underway:** **4 tons per diem** (per Captain Macfarlane's report on the delivery voyage — this is a solid, directly-quoted figure).
- **Delivery voyage from Scotland (12 July – 11 September 1873, 59 days, non-stop):** Departed Glasgow with **280 tons of coal** aboard (well above the normal 60-ton bunker capacity — extra coal was stowed for the long ocean passage, since she neither coaled nor put into any port for the entire 59 days). Arrived in Fremantle with **30 tons** remaining in her bunkers.
- Note for Godfrey: the "260" figure his creator may have half-remembered belongs to the engine's peak horsepower, not coal tonnage — worth being precise about the distinction if a visitor asks.

## Propulsion

- **Propeller:** Single screw, 4 solid blades, 8 ft 6 in diameter, 11 ft 6 in pitch.
- **Spare propeller:** Uncommonly for a vessel her size, she carries a spare propeller stowed on deck.
- **Shaft:** 36 ft long, approx. 3½ in diameter.

## Speed

- **Normal cruising speed:** 7 knots (Court of Inquiry testimony, Nov 1873).
- **Delivery voyage average (59 days, continuous steam, Glasgow to Fremantle):** approx. 6.4 knots — described at the time as the fastest passage on record from England to Western Australia.
- On her final voyage, she made 5 knots from leaving Bunbury until around 8pm, then 7 knots afterward by patent log, before conditions deteriorated overnight.

## Hull, Rig and Fittings

- **Construction:** Composite — iron frames, clinker (lap-riveted) iron outer plating; not copper-sheathed, so she required regular hull cleaning and anti-fouling treatment.
- **Masts:** Two, pitch pine.
- **Colour:** Black hull above the waterline, white topsides, black funnel, red-brown anti-fouling composition below the waterline. Name "Georgette" in gilt letters on the bow.
- **Anchors:** Two bower anchors (approx. 2,096 lb and 297 lb), one stream anchor, one kedge. Chain cable 90 fathoms at 1 in; hempen stream cable 90 fathoms at 7½ in.
- **Boats:** Three — a lifeboat, a gig, and a pinnace (an early Lloyd's record shows two boats at build; a third was evidently added in a later refit).
- **Compass:** A recurring, documented problem. Deviated by up to 25 degrees on some headings — first flagged at the 1873 Murray Reef inquiry, and readjusted in Adelaide in August 1875. This was a known vulnerability Godfrey would have been well aware of.

## Accommodation

- Built originally as a cargo vessel, not for passengers. Refitted after purchase by Connor & McKay with two passenger cabins of twenty berths each, and two saloons — one for ladies, one for gentlemen. The refit was praised on her 1873 arrival for cleanliness and comfort.

## Commercial Facts

- **Purchase price:** The manuscript's figure is £9,000 for the vessel, with a further £7,000–£8,000 spent on passenger-accommodation alterations, for a total outlay of £16,000–£17,000. **[DISCREPANCY]** A separate research source (citing Colonial Secretary's records via McCarthy) gives a purchase price of £14,000 — possibly a different accounting of the same transaction, or a later valuation. Recommend using the manuscript's £9,000 + refit figure as the primary answer, since it's the one that made it into the book.
- **Government mail subsidy:** £4,000 per year.
- **Gross monthly earnings (1875 prospectus):** approximately £1,000/month (~£12,000/year).
- **Insurance on the vessel at time of loss:** £1,000 (roughly 7% of purchase price, per Court of Inquiry testimony).
- **Agents:** Messrs King & Son, Fremantle.

## Service History Godfrey Would Have Known

- **First WA arrival:** 11 September 1873, anchoring at Sulphur Bay, Garden Island (not Fremantle Harbour itself) for engine cleaning and hull scraping.
- **Murray Reef stranding**, October 1873 — grounded near Fremantle a month after arrival; saved when the engineer forgot to reverse the engines and the still-turning screw and set sails carried her clear. At least two holes punched through the hull, rudder lost, steered to shore on a jury rig. Captain Wilson found guilty of want of due care; his master's certificate was found to have been informally and improperly granted.
- **Warnbro Sound stranding**, November 1873 — a second grounding within weeks.
- **Sequence of masters before Godfrey:** Macfarlane (delivery voyage) → Wilson (Murray Reef) → Gordon McKinnon (popular; farewelled with 40 sovereigns and a gold chronometer watch, left March 1875) → R. Shand (died suddenly of paralysis at sea off Hamelin Bay, November 1875 — almost exactly where the Georgette herself would be lost a year later) → Godfrey, chief officer from mid-1876, took command roughly a month before the final voyage.
- **The Catalpa pursuit, April 1876** — the Georgette's role as the unwilling pursuer of the American whaleship *Catalpa* during the Fenian prisoner escape from Fremantle.
- **Total complement on the final voyage (1 December 1876):** 72 souls — 22 crew and 50 passengers (27 men, 13 women, 10 children). Three boats were readied that night: the lifeboat, the gig, and a smaller pinnace.
