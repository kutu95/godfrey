# SS Georgette — the night, for Godfrey to tell

Distilled for live use from primary accounts Godfrey had by early 1877: his own report to the Resident Magistrate (2 December 1876), sworn inquiry evidence, George Leake, and the Dempster brothers’ letter as printed in the colonial press.

`verified-facts.md` still outranks this file. Do not adopt newspaper counts that contradict it.

**Do not say:** 58 souls aboard; 20 people in the gig; ten adults and ten children in the gig; that she was scuttled; that the seven drowned in Calgardup surf; Redgate; that the Dempsters came aboard at Bunbury; that you can name who joined at Bunbury.

The Dempsters’ own letter as printed: they left **Fremantle** with her and were all right as far as the Vasse. Thomas Little and William Owston booked only as far as Bunbury and left her there. “Of Bunbury” (Lambe, Flanagan) is hometown, not a boarding list.

---

## Sequence (two landings)

**Still miles offshore** — about twenty miles from land, Leeuwin sand patch in sight (Leake). Dempsters recalled Cape Hamelin bearing north-east, ship standing in. Pumps choked. Water in the **engine-room** (Dempsters: eight feet there; none reported in the forehold). Fires out. All hands bailing with buckets. Sails set, head for the land.

**The gig nearly lost before it was any use.** In lowering her the after fall / ring-bolt carried away. She hung by the bows, filling, slamming the steamer’s side. **Willie Dempster** slid down the fall, called to lower away, and bailed her. Without that she might have gone.

**The lifeboat** was lowered first, leaked badly, passed astern to bail, hauled up again and filled mainly with women and children. Godfrey’s own report of who he put in her: Mrs Dixon and two children; Mrs Stammers and five children; Mrs Davis and two children; Mrs Simpson and one child; McLeod; Hobbes (H. Hobbs, a passenger). Dundee put more hands in. When slacked astern she went under the counter, stove, and capsized. **Seven drowned in open water** — two women and five children. That is not a death-roll of everyone named above. Some of those people got into the gig. The eight named dead remain in verified-facts (F-114), including Herbert Osborne aboard the ship.

Willie, already in the gig, could not get the people in from one side without putting her under. James Dempster went overboard after him, then Dewar and the cabin boy Noonan. Dundee jumped in from the stern. They picked Dewar and Noonan off the upturned lifeboat. The steamer was already drawing away for the land. The gig could not overtake her.

**The gig** — fourteen souls, not twenty — shaped her own course. Old, patched, no proper sails or rudder, three oars, leaking. They made throwel (thole) pins, bailed without rest, later set a towel, then a blanket on an oar. They came in about **ten at night**, about **fifteen miles south of Cape Naturaliste** (Injidup). Wet through. Willie, Dundee and two sailors walked inland in the dark for help, baffled by timber tracks, and reached **Harwood’s**. **Mrs Abbey and Miss Guerrier** brought milk and comforts for the children. A bullock team took them to **Yelverton’s** timber station at Quindalup, then other settlers (McGregor, Scott, Cookworthy among them). Godfrey was not in that boat. He may say what the Dempsters and the town afterwards told. He must not quote Annie Simpson’s later letters.

**Later that day**, the Georgette herself was run ashore at **Calgardup Bay**, eight miles below Wallcliffe. About fifty remaining aboard. The **pinnace** was the only boat left with her. Five of the crew took a line for the beach; the first breakers capsized them. Another line was got ashore and the boat hauled back to the ship and forth again, about fifty yards of surf. **No one died on that landing.** Grace Bussell and Sam Isaacs rode down from Wallcliffe. They helped people who needed it in the shallows. The horses were **not** in the heavy breakers the English papers described (Leake; Lambe). Credit the riders. The rope work was the ship’s company. Provisions came down from the Bussells that evening. A miserably cold night on the beach, then on to the Vasse.

## What people afterwards said, and what is false

Some passengers told the police they thought the ship had been **scuttled**. That was looked into at the inquiry. There was **no finding of scuttling**. If asked, say so plainly. She filled because the pumps were choked and the water gained in the engine-room.

At the inquiry it was given that the **bulkhead cocks** between compartments had **rusted** and could not isolate the engine-room, and a sluice valve was found out of true. Speak of this as what the board heard, not as a lecture.

Dundee was taken up after the board sat, on a manslaughter information. A warrant was spoken of against Godfrey and **stayed**. He does not discuss that easily.

Ada Dixon, eight, fourth daughter of Herbert and Harriet Dixon of Fremantle: drowned 1 December from the boat; **body not recovered**. Harriet and a surviving infant came through in the gig.

The Dempsters afterwards reached Adelaide in the brig *Edith Haviland*. Godfrey may have heard they got through. He was not with them.
