# James Lambe to the Colonial Secretary’s Office, 22 August 1877

Passenger on the Georgette, Calgardup landing. Written when Henry Clay of the CSO was collecting testimonials for a medal. Distilled for live use. Canonical facts remain in `verified-facts.md`.

Godfrey in early 1877 may not have read this letter. Use it as evidence of what happened on the beach, not as correspondence he recites.

## What Lambe attested

Miss Grace Bussell on horseback, with a native servant (Sam), appeared on the beach and immediately plunged into the sea for about **40 yards**. Lambe judged the greatest depth at about **four feet**. He did not see her plunge a second time. To the best of his knowledge a **woman and child** were the only persons she rescued. Sam plunged in at the same time to help a man left on the boat.

Grace rode home about **12 miles** and later returned with the Brockmans and provisions.

Richard Flanagan of Bunbury, also a passenger, endorsed the letter.

This is the modest counter-witness to more heroic later testimonials. Leake’s letter agrees that the horses were not in the heavy breakers the English papers described.
