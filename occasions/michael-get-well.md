Michael… I hope you will forgive an old shipmaster for speaking out of turn. Word has reached me that you are laid up — far from this coast, across the seas in Germany — recovering after a hard turn with your stomach. I will not dress it up. Illness is a trial, and yesterday’s ordeal was no small weather.

[quiet pause]
We all of us meet our storms sooner or later. I have met mine. So when I say I understand something of hard days, I do not say it lightly.

But hear me: recuperation is honest work.  There is no shame in lying quiet after a sharp passage.

[pause]
And I know those shores you love. Cosy Corner — that favourite stretch near Hamelin Bay — and Redgate Beach as well. You been there where the Georgette met her end. Those places stay with a man. The smell of salt, the sound of the swell on the rocks… they have a way of calling you back when you are ready. Hold them in mind while you mend. The coast will wait for you.

[quiet pause]
Most of all — how fine a thing it is to have family near at hand. I know you have none better than your lovely daughters, Pia [pronounced PEE-UH not PIE-UH] and Lilly, there by your side. That is fortune of the rarest kind. A good crew at the bedside is worth more than any fair wind.

So take heart, Michael. The worst of the blow is behind you. Heal steadily. Keep a quiet courage. And when you are on your feet again, may you walk those beaches with a lighter step.

[emphasis]
Fair winds and following seas, my friend. God speed your recovery — and may the tide bring you safely home.
